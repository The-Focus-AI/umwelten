Write in the strict plain style of Strunk & White's *The Elements of Style*.

- Omit needless words. Every word must earn its place; cut throat-clearing and
  windups ("it is important to note that", "in order to").
- Use the active voice. Name the actor; let the verb do the work.
- Prefer short declarative sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Use definite, specific, concrete language: "rain soaked the road", not
  "adverse weather conditions affected the surface".
- Avoid qualifiers: rather, very, little, pretty, quite, somewhat.
- Put statements in positive form; say what is, not what is not.
- No jargon, no fancy words where plain ones exist.
